THE SPHERE · OBSERVATORY / 0.8

A place to test what the sky can be.

Fly through a mathematical Dyson shell. Set a viewpoint, move the shade, and save a photograph whose conditions you can reproduce.

Start here

Choose a camera destination; world, time, exposure, lens and quality settings are preserved. Camera → Complete scene studies explicitly loads staged conditions. Drag the image to look around. Use W A S D to fly and Q / E to move down / up; hold Shift for faster travel. The mouse wheel changes speed. Use the Camera panel to jump to exact coordinates. The centre marker reports the first object in that direction.

Find the nearby ground

Altitude measures the ground below you, not the world in your view. Camera → Inspect surface below points at your local surface. The live view reports the approximate footprint of a pixel near its centre. Regional atlas adds filtered, world-anchored detail as it becomes visible; Original v0.1 material preserves the first visual treatment.

Measure an area

Choose Measure area, then drag a rectangle or trace a freehand outline. The result integrates the visible curved shell and excludes openings, shades and the star. One Earth surface includes all land and ocean (about 510 million km²). Sampling difference compares two resolutions; it is not a guaranteed error bound. Save measurement keeps your outline, camera and result.

Make a motion study

Capture → Record 4K SDR clip captures a 12-second local video and its settings. First light stages an accelerated eclipse-edge transition; Under the shade adds a slow look and moving plate. Your chosen surface style is retained. Keep this tab visible while recording. Frame timing depends on your browser and GPU.

What is measured

The shell and stellar disk are analytic spheres. The breach is a circular cap with an optional fractured boundary; diameter is nominal when fractured. The shade is a thin disk with an optional notch, moving along a straight, actively controlled path. Camera positions use double precision; rendering uses normalized, camera-relative calculations. The star's apparent size and visible fraction are measured independently on the CPU.

What is approximate

Surface patterns are illustrative reflectance maps, not generated habitable terrain. In Collection study, ShellShine estimates a coloured first bounce from 192 sampled surface regions. It is not a converged multiple-bounce solution. Original studies retain uniform fill. The optional atmosphere is a simple local scattering approximation, not a climate or radiative-transfer model. External stars are a deterministic illustrative field, not a catalogue. Photograph exposure and tone mapping are artistic, not calibrated photometry. Shadows near a shade edge use finite sampling.

Physical boundaries

Artificial gravity, shell support, atmospheric containment, and shade propulsion are assumed. Flight is a free camera, not orbital mechanics. This version keeps the camera inside the shell and outside the star; a breach provides a view outward. Thin-shell and no-refraction approximations apply. Low-altitude material views remain a smooth mathematical surface; surface walking is outside scope.

Your data stays here

No network requests, telemetry, subscriptions, or runtime AI. Bookmarks live in this browser. Export a scene or photograph ZIP to keep a portable copy. Imported files are validated. All source code ships with this app.
