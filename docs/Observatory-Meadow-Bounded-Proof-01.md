## 1. Decision and scope

Retain WebGL2, the custom renderer, World Address v2, the existing terrain/collision owners and local coordinate/depth contracts. Replace or substantially revise the current grass population, blade geometry, distance representations and shading. There is no Three.js rewrite, WebGPU prerequisite, Grassworks dependency purchase or Blender/glTF pipeline prerequisite.

There are two distinct exits:

- **B1 — Beautiful bounded meadow:** a fixed, geographically anchored study passes actual-app visual, animation, frame-budget and export checks. B1 is an internal IB-075 gate, not a new programme milestone and not M1 acceptance.
- **M1 — Endless Meadow:** after B1, the same accepted appearance survives a moving residency window, geographic boundaries, long travel, return, rebase and disposal. Only IB-075-K can re-establish M1 and close IB-075.

The bounded study has a **256 × 256 m geographic coverage domain**, with a **32 × 32 m central viewing/walking area**. The coverage domain stays fixed in the world when the camera moves. It is not a moving circular carpet. Individual blades are generated only in the nearby representation window, not over all 65,536 m² at once. A deterministic 16 m ecological blend at the domain boundary joins existing surroundings; the central proof route does not reach that boundary. A separate boundary inspection must show a deliberate meadow/woodland transition rather than a missing-residency ring.

The minimum meadow ground material, ecological tree-placement override, lighting bridge and canopy transition are explicitly included in IB-075. Full terrain clipmaps, metre-scale terrain remeshing, new hydrology, a new character controller, other biome conversions, new sky art and a general foliage-authoring pipeline remain outside this proof. IB-080 must inherit this material work rather than be a prerequisite for it.

## 3. The visual contract

Use [the supplied grass reference](evidence/meadow-design-01/reference-reddit.png) for botanical appearance, coverage and depth. It is not an asset to map onto the scene. Match the visual qualities, not unverified assumptions about its exact camera, density or implementation.

| Aspect | Required appearance | Rejection signal |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground | Overlapping, individually readable tapered blades; ground largely hidden beneath a canopy | Olive floor with isolated strokes; a few dense tufts separated by large bare gaps |
| Blade form | Continuous curvature, tapered tips, varied lean, width and height | Rectangular strips, duplicated coplanar faces, identical vertical sticks |
| Depth and colour | Darker bases, restrained green/gold variation, lighter tips and plausible backlit edges | Uniform luminous lines, flat constant colour, unrelated grass and ground palettes |
| Wind | Coherent gusts crossing neighbouring plants, with smaller independent flutter | Random thrashing, synchronized walls, moving roots or sliding far-ground slabs |
| Distance | The same canopy simplifies into clumps, then matching ground response | Visible rings, sudden thinning, rectangular tiles, colour bands or shimmer |
| Composition | Open foreground, deliberate woodland edges; actual Sphere lighting and backdrop | Forest props throughout the supposed meadow; a replacement sky hiding integration defects |

Start with blades 0.35–0.9 m high, a small minority approaching 1.1 m, and approximately 3–12 mm base widths. These are art starting values. Use 4–6 vertical segments with a pointed or very narrow tip, a controlled curve and height-dependent bend. Maintain realistic proportions while measuring projected visibility. Do not hide insufficient coverage by widening every blade into a broad leaf.

Start at **250 blades/m² in fully eligible near-field meadow**, with an agreed art-tuning range of 200–300 blades/m² for the first proof preset. Use stratified or otherwise bounded-gap placement, with low-frequency variations in lean, height and colour; avoid random uniform planting that leaves accidental holes. A botanical tuft may contain several blades, but must have a plausible footprint measured in centimetres, not fourteen blades across a four-metre disk.

Visual acceptance requires the user's explicit B1 review of the delivered images/clip. All implementation, numerical, performance and export work needed for that review should be completed before requesting acceptance. Ordinary tasks A–H do not introduce intermediate permission gates.

## 5. Geographic placement and terrain ownership

Use a fixed chart tied to the canonical saved Open meadow direction and a deterministic tangent basis, independent of `terrainAnchor`. Within the bounded domain use half-open **8 × 8 m logical rendering cells** identified by integer coordinates. The fixed chart is geographic content; a camera-relative render frame is temporary. Do not generate a camera grid and snap its centres into level-30 cube cells, which currently aliases multiple requests to one patch key.

Each generated root has the canonical World Address v2 location plus a stable identity derived from coverage/profile revision, fixed chart/cell coordinates and candidate number. Additional botanical blades derive from that stable root ID and blade number, never an index in a filtered array. Compute actual cell surface area when mapping chart coordinates to the shell so density is measured per square metre. Half-open ownership and canonical boundary rules eliminate duplicated edge candidates. Include the world, representation, terrain and ecological revisions in content-cache identity.

For B1 this is an explicitly bounded fixed chart. C must establish cell-edge ownership and rebase invariance here; it must not claim to have certified an unlimited global atlas. J extends the same stable-content principle to canonical geographic pages/cell neighbours at production radius, with cross-page/cube-face ownership and declared distortion limits before M1. No increase of World Address's maximum level is required merely to name deterministic microcell candidates inside a parent region.

Use one production terrain-support adapter for roots, patch centres, bounds, shading normals and camera height. It returns the height and normal of the actual rendered/collision surface, using the owning triangle or equivalent exact interpolation. Sampling a separate coarse analytic height is insufficient if it disagrees with the visible mesh. Use `radius - terrainHeight` for inner-shell radial placement. Resolve local frames once, subtract origins in CPU doubles before float packing, and use the same basis for culling, projection and lighting.

Every tuft child samples its own ground position on slopes. Neighbouring normal samples must query neighbouring geography/triangles, not reuse the parent direction and semantic sample. Check low/high terrain, flat ground, sloped triangles, patch borders and rebases. Target root-to-support error at most **2 mm** in the bounded proof; report maximum errors and numerical precision. Do not compensate for burial with an arbitrary large depth bias. Culling bounds include the actual prototype extent, height, maximum bend and every child offset.

## 6. Representation and rendering architecture

The following are initial distances for the 80° proof lens. Adjust them from projected silhouette size while keeping the selected density and frame budget explicit.

| Distance | Representation | Required relationship |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12 m | Full-density segmented blades | Readable individual curves, stable roots and continuous canopy |
| 12–20 m | Overlap between blades and canopy clumps | Deterministic thinning and replacement preserve silhouette area and colour |
| 20–64 m | Cheaper clump geometry or actual alpha-cutout clump atlas | Many-blade volume, not one thin rectangle per old root |
| Beyond about 64 m | Ground-integrated grass colour/normal/roughness and broad wind response | Same coverage/wetness field; no floating far-material cards |

LOD distance is evaluated against the actual camera and local cell/root bounds; a 256 m patch centre must not classify a blade at the observer's feet. Keep a readable canopy during wide-lens, grazing and downward views. A single scalar crossfade sum of one is not evidence of equivalent coverage.

Procedural ribbon geometry is sufficient for near blades. Middle clumps can be procedural mesh clusters or a small independently authored/baked alpha atlas. If alpha-cutout cards are chosen, provide real UVs, silhouette alpha, suitable orientation, mipmaps and edge filtering. A textureless constant-alpha card does not qualify. Record atlas provenance and packaging; this does not depend on IB-090's general glTF pipeline. Prefer stable alpha testing/coverage with measured overdraw over per-frame sorting of thousands of transparent blades. Screen-space noise alone must not cause visible shimmer or inconsistent export seams.

Bridge the vegetation pass to the renderer's existing star direction, star visibility/shadowing, cavity fill, exposure and atmospheric composition. Convert directions to the vegetation frame consistently. Bend normals with geometry. Add base occlusion/darkening, controlled tip colour, a restrained specular response and inexpensive two-sided transmission. These are appearance approximations, not a requirement for full path tracing or per-blade ray tracing. Honour the world's light changes, Shade occlusion and attack state.

Integrate the far canopy into the real ground material. Use coverage, wetness, blade palette and a shared low-frequency wind field to modulate its appearance; never translate terrain-scale quads to simulate wind. Maintain the existing ground's depth/collision ownership. The study must work with the normal material/shadow/antialiasing settings, not only after disabling them.

## 7. Residency, frame work and preparation

Retain immutable prototypes and packed typed-array cell records. Generate roots in a worker when a geographic cell becomes wanted; prepare/upload completed cells within bounded per-frame budgets. Keep the old valid support and presentation until the replacement is ready. Cancellation, late worker replies and synchronous export preparation follow the same versioned content keys.

A settled draw uses retained buffers plus camera, light and wind uniforms. Do not rebuild botanical child arrays, serialize addresses, hash every root, sort every instance or repack camera-relative origins each frame. Maintain stable generation order. Compute diagnostic content hashes on generation/revision changes, or explicit diagnostic requests; ordinary telemetry must not become an O(blade-count) frame task.

Camera movement within a settled cell changes uniforms and visibility; it must not upload all origins. View rotation can change draw commands without regenerating cells. Actual additions/removals update only affected slots/buffers. A floating-origin rebase changes a local transform or performs a bounded, explicitly measured rebase operation without moving world roots. Instance-buffer cache keys and invalidation must include every render-relevant content property, including orientation/colour, not just position/scale.

Do not generate individual blade roots for far-only cells. Keep inexpensive semantic/ground descriptors there. Reuse prepared draw groups with conservative bounds and a small number of material variants. Release buffers/worker packets after residency grace periods and on teardown; leaving a scene must dispose its vegetation resources without disturbing pinned terrain support.

## 8. Wind, pause and captures

Wind is an address-stable spatial field with a common direction, slowly travelling gusts and restrained per-blade flutter. The new profile defines its unit convention once: wind bend is a fraction of blade height, not kilometre displacement. Defaults in validation, workers and shader uniforms must agree.

Use one wind-phase evaluator across blades, clumps and ground response. Its spatial coordinates belong to the fixed geographic field, so rebase and LOD changes do not reset gust phase. Ordinary wind is transient appearance; it never changes root identity or collision support.

Define a versioned mapping from scene time to wind phase, with a modest visual rate independent of the application's 60×/600× accelerated astronomical playback. Use `phaseSeconds = anchorPhaseSeconds + (sceneTime - anchorSceneTime) / sceneSecondsPerWindSecond`. For normal playback set the positive divisor from the selected simulation time rate and the profile's visual wind speed, so one real second advances approximately one wind second at the default visual speed. On a time-rate change, resolve the current phase first, store it and the current scene time as the new anchors, then change the divisor. Save the mapping alongside the resolved phase. Given those values and scene time, evaluation is deterministic. Explicit timeline scrubbing uses the recorded mapping and may intentionally select another wind pose; normal play/pause or time-rate changes must not jump backward. G must cover zero/invalid rates through validation and keep legacy scenes on their legacy evaluator.

Normal pause stores the phase of the last displayed frame. Capture snapshots that same resolved phase before pausing/export preparation; all photograph tiles and panorama faces share it. Resume continues from that phase. Legacy `windClock`/`pausedClock` fields retain their old scene interpretation until explicitly converted. Test playback via the actual play control or `setPlaying`; do not assume `setState({playing:true})` changes the playing flag.

These are design acceptance targets to implement and measure, not achieved results. If the approved visual target cannot fit, record the tradeoff and revise the preset explicitly; do not silently lower density or internal resolution to turn a failure into a pass.

| Budget | B1 target |
|---|---|
| Hardware and browser | Production RTX 5080, normal supported browser; record exact GPU/driver/browser/build |
| Primary preview | Fixed **1920 × 1080 internal canvas**, 80° horizontal FOV, 60 fps selected; adaptive scaling off for certification |
| Normal rendering settings | Detailed materials, production shadows/AA, intended atmosphere; same settings in off/on pairs |
| Full-frame CPU submission | p95 at most 6 ms after settling |
| Incremental grass CPU | p95 at most 2 ms relative to matched meadow-off baseline |
| Full-frame GPU | p95 at most 14 ms; incremental grass GPU p95 at most 4 ms when timer data is valid |
| Preview delivery | Sustained at least 58 presented/drawn frames/s on the recorded 60 fps route; p95 interval at most 18.5 ms; no warm interval above 33.4 ms |
| Detailed-blade capacity | At most 500,000 resident detailed records and 250,000 detailed instances submitted per normal view; allow whole-cell residency without silently lowering near density |
| Vegetation draw calls | Target at most 32 per normal preview view, measured with actual culling/LOD |
| Vegetation GPU residency | At most 32 MiB including instances, prototypes and clump textures; report temporary peaks separately |
| Vegetation CPU residency | At most 64 MiB including retained packets and metadata; measure heap separately from typed-array bytes |
| Stationary settled uploads | Zero instance/prototype bytes per frame, including animated wind |
| Walking preparation/upload | At most 2 ms main-thread preparation and 2 MiB upload in a warm frame; expose queue depth/latency |
| Existing ground residency | Preserve its independent 64 MiB production budget and protected support |

At 250 blades/m² a fully detailed 15 m radius would contain approximately 177,000 blades and 5.4 MiB of 32-byte records. Whole 8 m cells intersecting the nearby window retain additional candidates, so residency and submitted-instance caps are deliberately distinct. The resident cap permits approximately 15.3 MiB of detailed records before prototypes, middle clumps and textures. This is sizing arithmetic, not an instruction to allocate all candidates across the entire domain or a performance guarantee. The 12–20 m overlap must use stable candidate subsets/proxy substitution so the submitted cap and visual coverage agree; LOD changes do not rename roots. GPU bytes alone do not describe JavaScript object cost.

Capture cold preparation/compilation separately. Warm one view, then record three sequential 15-second windows at a stationary view, a 2.8 m/s outward/return walk inside the central area, and a controlled turn/elevation inspection. Repeat the same poses and scene/light times with vegetation disabled. Record CPU median/p95/max, GPU distributions with disjoint-query validation, all frame intervals, uploads, allocations, draw counts and memory. Also report the user's native viewport and any adaptive scale as a separate practical run; it cannot replace fixed-resolution evidence.

Bound each run and stop a failing test instead of looping a sustained overloaded scene. Invalid/unavailable GPU timers are recorded as unavailable; that hardware pass remains uncertified for the GPU criterion until measured by a valid mechanism. Do not equate CPU submission, forced `gl.finish()` timing and GPU elapsed time. The 4K photograph and panorama are quality/export checks, not 60 fps requirements. Keep their output resolution independent of preview scaling.
