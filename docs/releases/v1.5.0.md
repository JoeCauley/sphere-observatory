# Observatory v1.5.0

This release brings the current Watershed, ground-material and Shade-body work into the downloadable Observatory. The project README is a full guide to the world and its exploration tools. Seventeen fresh 4K photographs replace the public gallery, including the reference polar-court composition, two biomes, six Watershed altitudes, three regional compositions, intact and broken Shade edges, a Wound, the stellar conservatory and clouds.

## Explore this build

- **Watersheds:** connected neighbourhood geography with receiving-lake, quiet-reach and open-meadow compositions; regional materials, dry-bank arrivals and preserved geography/art revisions.
- **Ground materials:** local terrain receives the same biome, era and Wound settings as the surrounding shell, including the corrected snow, desert and machinery surfaces.
- **Solid Shades:** continuous faces 180 m apart, finished intact perimeters, exposed damaged boundaries, coarse/fine edge closure, automatic artwork preparation, camera clearance and saved-scene compatibility.
- **Shade image quality:** boundary classification and material reduction preserve thin coverage and mean radiance across texture levels.
- **Project presentation:** a purpose-led README, importable scenes and original 4K images. The earlier gallery and all previous documentation images are preserved in a hash-checked archive. App texture assets remain available to the renderer.

## Download and run

Extract `sphere-observatory-1.5.0.zip`. With Node.js 20 or later installed, open **Launch Observatory.cmd** on Windows, or run `npm start` from the extracted folder. Visit [localhost:8766](http://127.0.0.1:8766/). Use **Stop Observatory.cmd** or Ctrl+C when finished.

A hardware-accelerated WebGL 2 browser is required. There is no application compilation step or runtime dependency installation. See the [full guide](../../README.md) and [photograph gallery](../../examples/observatory/README.md).

The Shade-boundary suite ran in coverage-only mode. It does not certify cold-capture equality. The hero, Ultra Desert and cloud gallery views had earlier nonmatching frame pairs before settling; every comparison remains in the gallery metadata.

The gallery records the actual source fingerprint, GPU, dimensions, image hashes and every adjacent-frame comparison. A prepared scene must produce two identical final frames before the helper accepts a photograph. The 4K originals are unchanged renderer output; JPEGs are smaller display copies.

## Known limits

The strict cold-capture diagnostic remains open: earlier Shade-boundary testing found two changed pixels at one channel level. Passing prepared gallery captures does not establish universal cold-frame equality. The renderer has not been altered to hide this result.

Regional Watershed materials and water are analytic representations; detailed shore environments across all three compositions remain future work. Most of the Sphere is not authored walking terrain. Complete Shade-face environments, full physical light transport, engineering feasibility and general orbital dynamics are not claimed. Cold preparation and frame-time outliers remain performance work.

Older portable scenes retain their saved revisions. The hero intentionally preserves the reference camera and authored polar landmark while using current source and Shade bodies.
